The Story

A line, drawn by hand

Adhikari Razan didn't fall into barbering — he chased it. What began as cutting friends' hair in a borrowed chair became a study in millimetres: where a fade should break, how a part should sit, when to stop.

Every session is a ritual, not a transaction. A read of the face, a conversation about what you want to project, then the work — patient, deliberate, and finished only when it's right. No two heads are the same, so no two cuts are either.

The result is a chair people travel across Kathmandu for, and a signature cut that's become its own reason to book.
